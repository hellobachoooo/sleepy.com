Full body A

About 30 minutes. Rest 60 to 90 seconds between sets.

  • Bodyweight squats3 × 10–12
  • Push-ups (knees down if needed)3 × 6–10
  • Backpack rows (bent over, books in a bag)3 × 10
  • Glute bridges3 × 12
  • Plank3 × 20–30 sec
Stop an exercise if you feel sharp pain. ▶ Watch: beginner form guide

Full body B

About 30 minutes. Same rest as day A.

  • Reverse lunges3 × 8 each leg
  • Incline push-ups (hands on a desk or bench)3 × 8–12
  • Chair or bench dips3 × 6–10
  • Standing calf raises3 × 15
  • Side plank2 × 15–20 sec each side
When every set feels easy, add a rep or two before making it harder in other ways.

Cardio + core

About 30 minutes. You should be able to talk, but not sing.

  • Brisk walk or easy jog20 min
  • Dead bug3 × 8 each side
  • Bird dog3 × 8 each side
  • Mountain climbers (slow and controlled)3 × 20 sec
New to running? Alternate 1 minute of jogging with 2 minutes of walking. ▶ Watch: core moves

Warm-up and cool-down

Spend 5 minutes before and after every session.

  • March on the spot or skip1 min
  • Arm circles10 each way
  • Hip circles and leg swings10 each side
  • Cool-down: slow walk, then gentle stretches3–5 min
If you have a heart, joint or other health condition, check with a doctor before starting a new routine.

A simple eating plan

No strict diet. Aim for regular meals built the same way each time.

Vegetables
Protein
Grains

A rough guide for lunch and dinner: half vegetables, a quarter protein, a quarter whole grains.

Breakfast

Eggs, or sprouts, or poha with peanuts. Add a fruit. Oats with milk or curd also work.

Lunch

Roti or rice, dal or paneer or chicken, a vegetable dish and some salad.

Snack

Roasted chana, fruit, nuts, curd or a boiled egg. Easy to carry between classes.

Dinner

Similar to lunch but lighter. Try to finish eating a couple of hours before bed.

Drink water through the day. Go easy on sugary drinks and late-night snacks. Swap any food above for something that suits your diet, culture or allergies.

Common questions

Should I work out in the morning or evening?

Whichever you will actually stick to. Very hard sessions close to bedtime can make it harder to fall asleep for some people.

Do I need protein shakes?

Most students can get enough protein from regular food such as eggs, dal, paneer, curd, chicken, fish or soy.

What if I miss a session?

Skip the guilt and do the next one on schedule. A plan you can repeat beats a perfect plan you drop.

When should I talk to a professional?

If you have a medical condition, feel dizzy or have chest pain when exercising, or want advice on weight or food, speak to a doctor or a registered dietitian.
