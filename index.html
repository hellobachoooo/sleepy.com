Sleep well, study better

A first-year guide to getting enough rest when your schedule is anything but regular.

Plan your bedtime

Why sleep is part of studying

Your brain does real work while you rest. Cutting sleep to fit in more study often backfires.

Memory

Sleep helps your brain store what you learned during the day. Notes you read before bed tend to stick better than notes you read at 4 a.m.

Focus

Even one short night makes it harder to pay attention and to stay patient in a long lecture.

Mood

Tired people feel stress more sharply. Good sleep makes the first weeks away from home easier to handle.

How much you need

Most adults need about 7 to 9 hours a night. Some 17 to 18 year olds may need a little more. ▶ Watch: Sleep is your superpower (TED)

Habits that help

Small, boring routines work better than any sleep hack.

  • ⏰
    Keep a steady wake-up timeTry to stay within an hour or so of the same time, even on weekends.
  • 📱
    Put the phone away before bedScreens keep your mind busy. Charge it across the room if you can.
  • ☕
    Watch caffeine late in the dayTea, coffee and energy drinks can stay in your body for hours. Many people cut them off by the afternoon. ▶ Watch: caffeine and sleep
  • 🌤️
    Get daylight in the morningLight early in the day helps your body clock know when it is time to sleep later.
  • 🛏️
    Keep your bed for sleepStudy at a desk or in the library so your brain links the bed with rest.

Bedtime planner

Pick the time you need to wake up. You will see bedtimes that line up with the end of a sleep cycle.

A sleep cycle lasts about 90 minutes and falling asleep takes about 15 minutes. Both vary from person to person, so treat these as a starting point. ▶ Watch: how naps work

Common questions

Can I catch up on sleep at the weekend?

A long lie-in can help a little, but it also shifts your body clock and makes Monday harder. Small, regular amounts of sleep work better.

Is a nap a good idea?

A short nap of about 20 minutes in the afternoon can help you feel sharper. Long naps late in the day may make it harder to fall asleep at night. ▶ Watch: sleep cycles explained

What if I have an exam tomorrow?

Review lightly, then go to bed at a reasonable time. Being rested usually helps more than one more hour of cramming.

When should I talk to someone?

If you often cannot sleep, feel exhausted during the day, or snore loudly and stop breathing at night, speak with a doctor or your college health service.
